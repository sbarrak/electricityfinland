One clear answer first.

The dashboard answers one question a hundred times a day: is electricity cheap right now, and when will it be cheaper? Today that answer competes with more than eighty controls. These three mockups keep every feature and move the configuration out of the way, so the price leads.

A · Calm open B · Studio recommended C · Signal open

All three are working prototypes with example data. Open them on a phone, a tablet and a computer: the layout changes at each size. Tap bars, switch Spot / Total cost, open Explore, Alerts and Settings.

The problem

Everything has the same weight, so nothing leads.

The current page puts daily reading, analysis and one-time setup on one surface. Settings you change once a year sit above the chart you look at every hour.

Three directions

One structure, three characters.

All three use the same five sections and the same components. They differ in how the home screen reads and how much they show at once.

A · Calm

The price, in one sentence

Close to Apple Weather. A large current price, then one plain sentence: how it compares to today and when the cheap stretch starts. Below it are the day's bars, five facts and the cost planner. Everything else is one tap away.

  • Easiest to read at a glance, at any size
  • The sentence answers the real question without a chart
  • Lowest effort to build on the current code
  • Less on screen at once on a big monitor
  • Power users go to Explore more often

A workspace with an inspector

Close to Linear, Vercel and Stripe. The interface is monochrome, so the only colour on screen is the data. A sidebar holds the five sections, and one strip replaces the six cards.   • Shows the most without feeling packed
  • The breakdown and planner stay visible on a desktop
  • Explore turns the chart drop-down into named views
  • Scales to future features (new views, more pages)
  • More layout work: sidebar, rail and tab bar

C · Signal

The day as a clock

An instrument for deciding when to use power. The dial shows each quarter hour around the clock, with a hand at now. Next to it are the cheapest 1, 3 and 6 hours with a countdown. Below are the next 48 hours with wind and temperature. It is dark first (Nordic night), with a light theme too.

  • The most distinctive, and memorable on a wall tablet
  • "When should I run it?" answered by three cards
  • A dial is harder to read exactly than bars
  • Shows one day at a time

The new structure

Five sections instead of one long page.

Yes to sections, no to separate websites. Each section is a view in the same page (#now, #explore …), so switching is instant, the back button works, and links can point to a view. Each section answers one question.

Design rules

What makes it feel calm.

Answer before detail

The current price and one sentence come first. Charts, tables and analysis follow in that order.

Set-and-forget moves out

Limits, recipients, costs and servers change rarely. They get their own pages and stop crowding the daily view.

Colour means one thing

The chrome is neutral. Colour is reserved for the data, and every colour keeps the app's existing meaning:

publishedfixed, to comeestimate≥ high≤ low

Label the chart, not a legend

Limit lines say "Alert above 18". The cheapest window says "Cheapest 3 h". Each day shows its average. The 118-word legend becomes a short colour key.

One basis, everywhere

Spot or total cost, with or without VAT, is chosen once in the header and applies to every number. Alerts keep their own basis.

Built for thumbs

A tab bar within reach, 44 px targets, sheets that slide up from the bottom, and no hover-only information. Dark mode is designed, not inverted.

Recommendation

Build B, borrow from A and C.

B's structure gives the most room to grow and keeps the breakdown visible on a desktop. Put A's plain-language sentence under B's current price, and C's cheapest 1 / 3 / 6 h cards in the Now view. Keep the dial as an optional view in Explore.

  1. Tokens and componentsMove web/style.css to colour and spacing tokens (as in mockups/kit.css): segmented control, switch, sheet, group rows. No behaviour changes.
  2. Shell and sectionsAdd the sidebar, rail and tab bar with #now, #explore, #usage, #alerts and #settings. The existing elements move into sections; their ids and app.js handlers stay the same.
  3. Global price basisLift the Spot / Total and VAT controls into the header. The alarm row becomes Alerts → Price limits.
  4. ExploreTurn the Chart drop-down and the tabs into the views list. Chart options become the Layers sheet. Grid View becomes the Table view.
  5. Now viewHero price + sentence, the facts strip, the day chart, the inspector (breakdown) and the planner. The legend text becomes on-chart labels.
  6. PolishBottom sheets for dialogs, dark theme pass, reduced motion, keyboard focus, then test on an iPhone, an iPad and a desktop.
